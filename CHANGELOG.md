# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Added
- Portfolio stress scenarios in `src/portfolio.jl`: `CurveTwist` shifts the zero curve by different amounts at the short and long ends, the shift interpolated linearly in pillar time between the first and last pillars (equal ends are a parallel shift), applied with `twist_curve`; `HazardOverlay` layers a stylized credit overlay on the remaining cash flows, weighting each flow at time t by 1 - (1 - recovery) * (1 - exp(-hazard * t)), a form chosen so zero hazard is inert at any recovery and unit recovery is inert at any hazard, both exactly rather than approximately. `stressed_price` prices one instrument under the overlay, and `stress_scenario` runs a combined `StressScenario` on a single instrument or a whole portfolio and attributes the loss to the curve move alone, the modeled credit component alone, and their interaction, the three summing to the total loss. The overlay parameters are documented where they are defined as analyst-chosen model assumptions, explicitly not estimated from the January 2026 IILM auction record, which holds observed clearing rates and no default or recovery history. A new regression testset pins the acceptance against an independent oracle that restates the documented formulas: zero hazard and unit recovery reproduce the existing prices to the last bit, hazard 1e6 at zero recovery prices at exactly zero and at 40% recovery at 40% of the base price, stressed prices are monotone in hazard and in recovery, single-component scenarios attribute their whole loss to that component exactly, and on the reference fixture (twist +50bp short / -25bp long, hazard 8%, recovery 40%) the three-instrument portfolio falls from 3164.86 to 2753.36 per 1000 face, split 11.50 rate, 401.31 credit and -1.31 interaction.

### Added
- Batched curve pricing and a numerical benchmark suite in `src/analytics.jl`: `prepare_batch` lays every remaining cash flow of a portfolio out flat once, as year fractions and amounts, and `price_batch!` prices the whole portfolio into a preallocated vector with a reused discount buffer, so the hot loop allocates nothing; `price_batch` and a `ValuationContext` form wrap it, and `benchmark_pricing` runs the suite at three portfolio sizes with five repeats each, reporting Julia version, thread and BLAS settings, allocations and median seconds per instrument alongside the per-instrument baseline loop. `Project.toml` gains the `LinearAlgebra` standard library dependency for the BLAS report. Measured on this machine (Julia 1.13.1, 1 Julia thread, 2 BLAS threads, OpenBLAS): 10 instruments (100 cash flows) 0.22 microseconds per instrument against a 2.8 microsecond baseline, 100 instruments (1,000 cash flows) 0.21 microseconds against 2.87, 1,000 instruments (10,000 cash flows) 0.21 microseconds against 7.64, with 0 bytes allocated by every batched pass. A new regression testset pins the acceptance: batched prices agree with an independent BigFloat oracle to a worst error of 1.4e-14 per 100 face at all three sizes (bar 1e-8) and agree bit-for-bit with the per-instrument context path, the hot loop allocates exactly 0 bytes after warmup, and the benchmark report records the environment and both timings.

### Added
- Settlement-aware pricing in `src/analytics.jl`: a typed `ValuationContext` carrying the settlement date, the day count and the pricing basis, with `NominalYield` (nominal annual yield compounded `m` times per year) and `ZeroCurve` (continuously compounded rates) as distinct types so a bare number can never stand in for a convention. `price` returns the full price at settlement of the flows dated after it, `price_from_curve` values flows on a curve as of its anchor, and `price_decomposition` under a context reconciles clean plus accrued to the full price. `bump_pillar` in `src/curve.jl` moves one pillar by a given number of basis points, and `key_rate_dv01`, `key_rate_dv01s` and `curve_dv01` report the fall in the settlement full price per basis point at one pillar, at every pillar, and for a parallel shift; the key-rate figures sum to the parallel figure up to second-order cross terms. A new regression testset pins the acceptance: clean plus accrued equals dirty with accrued exactly 12.5 on 1000 face for the two-year semiannual fixture settled halfway through a period, yield and price round trips pass, analytic DV01 and convexity match central finite differences, settled flows (including a flow dated exactly on settlement) are excluded, and a pillar that touches neither the remaining flows nor the settlement factor leaves the price exactly unchanged.

### Fixed
- The stale-count guard in `test/runtests.jl` matched the digits 83 inside 483, so the suite failed on main once the README count reached 483. The guard now names the stale sentence ("is 83 tests") and passes; the README suite count is updated to 545.

### Added
- One versioned results schema (`sukuklab/results/1` in `examples/results.json`): Julia is now the single analytics authority for both renderers. `examples/make_results.jl` exports the playground pricing, the three cash-flow structures, and the UAE price sweep alongside the existing curve and valuation values, and records the generating Julia version (1.13) and the IILM source dates (auction 2026-01-13; sources published 2026-01-13 and 2026-03-05, all retrieved 2026-11-02). `examples/make_dashboard.jl` renders its snapshot from the schema instead of recomputing analytics inline, and `examples/make_dashboard.py` renders the price chart from the exported sweep instead of computing its own. A new regression test recomputes every schema value live from the package, and the Python parity checker independently recomputes the sweep from the shared fixture; chart payloads agree within 1e-8 per 1,000 face. CI pins Julia 1.13 and runs the dashboard generator.

### Added
- Golden fixtures pinning the schedule and day-count conventions to hand-computed values: a seven-month semiannual instrument (one regular period plus a one-month final stub paying its 30/360 fraction of 5 per 1,000 face, with accrued profit inside the stub accruing the stub distribution), and February-end to February-end 30/360 spans (2024-02-29 to 2025-02-28 is exactly one year under the bond-basis rules). The remaining acceptance clauses (month-end and leap-day schedules, duplicate pillar rejection, stub fractions) were audited against the existing fixtures and already hold.

### Added
- `calibration_report` in `src/curve.jl`: runs the sequential bootstrap and reports the outcome instead of only returning a curve or throwing. It returns a status (`:exact`, `:approximate` or `:infeasible`), the fitted curve (absent when infeasible), one repricing row per instrument (maturity, quoted price, model price, absolute residual, status), the largest residual, and a message naming the instrument that cannot be fitted when a quote set has no solution. The bootstrap's exactness scope is documented with it: it reprices the instruments it calibrates exactly, because each new pillar sits beyond every cash flow of the instruments already fitted; it is not a joint fit, and duplicate maturity quotes are rejected rather than averaged.
- Two curve conventions are now stated where the API is defined: the discount factor at the anchor is exactly 1 (the zero rate at t = 0 remains undefined), and interpolated forwards may be negative on a steeply falling curve and are reported rather than floored at zero.
- `LogDiscountCurve`, `SmoothCurve` and `interpolation_model_risk` in `src/curve.jl`: the fitted pillars read through two further interpolation schemes alongside the existing linear-zero curve (log-discount interpolation with piecewise constant forwards, and a monotone cubic on log discounts whose smoothness cannot manufacture a rising discount factor out of falling ones), plus a report comparing all three. The report states each scheme's exact repricing residuals on the calibrating instruments, leave-one-out pricing errors at held-out tenors (marking a pillar `:not_identified` when the reduced quote set cannot be refitted at all), sampled forward-rate ranges, zero rates at 2, 5 and 10 years, and 5y/10y reference prices under parallel shifts and single-pillar bumps. Negative forwards are governed by an explicit policy argument (`negative_forward_policy=:allow` reports them, `:reject` flags the scheme `:violates_policy`); no scheme floors or caps a forward under either policy. On the January 2026 IILM pillars the three schemes calibrate identically (residuals exactly 0) and imply only positive forwards, then diverge exactly where the data runs out: the 10y reference price is 104.58 (linear-zero), 107.94 (log-discount) and 111.98 (smooth) per 100 face, a 7.40 spread, while the largest held-out error inside the observed range is 0.38 per 100 face and no scheme wins at every held-out tenor.

### Fixed
- `ZeroCurve` construction now enforces what its documentation always claimed: pillars must be strictly increasing (a repeated pillar previously passed the sortedness check and later divided by the zero gap between the duplicates during interpolation), and pillars and zero rates must be finite (a NaN zero rate previously constructed fine and silently made every discount factor NaN).
- `bootstrap` validates the whole quote set before solving: an instrument with no cash flows (previously an indexing error when it was not the shortest instrument), cash flows out of date order or sharing a date, non-positive or non-finite cash-flow amounts, a cash flow dated before the anchor, a non-positive or non-finite quoted price, and duplicate maturity quotes (previously a silent duplicate pillar) are all rejected with explicit errors, and a quote with no solution is rejected naming the instrument's maturity.

### Added
- Contract-grade dated cash flows in `src/cashflows.jl`: schedules are modeled as explicit `DistributionPeriod`s (unadjusted contractual start and finish, payment date, regular/stub flag) generated from the anchor (`distribution_periods`), a `DayCount` enumeration (`Thirty360`, `Actual365`) alongside the existing symbols, a weekend business-day adjustment declared on the terms (`adjustment=:following`, `:modified_following` or `:preceding`; default `:unadjusted`), and an explicit payment-rounding policy on `payment_schedule` (default full precision; `rounding=:cent` rounds each payment to cents with the installment residual absorbed by the final payment).
- `price_decomposition` and `ytm_from_clean_price` in `src/analytics.jl`: a settlement price as three distinct quantities (full price, accrued profit, clean price) that reconcile exactly, and yield recovery from a quoted clean price.

### Fixed
- Schedule generation no longer steps month by month from an already-clamped date. A monthly schedule issued on the 30th previously paid on the 28th from March onward, and a semi-annual issued 2026-08-31 never landed on leap day 2028-02-29; dates are now computed from the issue anchor in one step, end-of-month anchors stay on month ends, and the spurious short final periods the drift created are gone.
- A final stub period previously paid a full regular distribution. It now pays its actual 30/360 fraction, and accrued profit inside a stub accrues that stub distribution.
- The 30/360 day count now follows the bond-basis rules: a second date on the 31st is kept when the first date is below the 30th (2026-01-29 to 2026-01-31 is 2/360, was 1/360), and February end-of-month dates count as the 30th (2026-02-28 to 2026-08-28 is 178/360, was 180/360; 2024-02-29 to 2024-08-29 is 179/360).
- The closed-form pricer rejected nothing: a maturity that is not a whole number of periods was silently rounded to the nearest period count. `sukuk_price` (and `sukuk_ytm` through it) now raises an error for non-whole-period maturity/frequency pairs and points at the dated schedule.
- Price presentation: the settlement present value was labeled a clean price in the README, the demo and the notebook even though it includes accrued profit. It is now presented as the full price, with accrued profit and the clean price stated separately (UAE retail T-Sukuk at settlement 2026-10-10: full 1000, accrued 1.41, clean 998.59 on 1000 face; no other quoted number changed).

### Added
- Published-terms reconstruction of the IILM auction record (`data/iilm_2026-01.json`): each of the four January 2026 series now carries its series number, ISIN, amount, clearing profit rate, actual issue date (2026-01-15) and published maturity date, the verbatim press-release quote, and its sources, with the auction date (2026-01-13) stored separately as the observation time.
- `src/marketdata.jl`: a validated loader (`load_auction_record`) for the auction record, with strict errors on missing or blank issue/maturity dates, unknown rate conventions, mixed issuers or currencies, and duplicate identifiers, plus model pricing under the record's named convention (simple profit rate, Actual/365) and per-series pricing residuals (`pricing_residuals`).
- `examples/make_iilm_residuals.jl`: regenerates the pricing-residual report (`examples/iilm_residuals.json` and `.csv`) with the source data file's SHA-256; all four residuals are 0.0 per 100 face.

## v1.1.0

### Fixed
- Curve bootstrapping now root-solves each new pillar on the full instrument
  present value using the candidate curve that already includes the new
  pillar, instead of discounting intermediate coupons on the old curve.
  With linear zero-rate interpolation, the new pillar changes discount
  factors for dates between the old and new pillars; freezing coupons on
  the old curve left a repricing error (~0.02/100 in the regression test).

### Tests
- New `bootstrap_reprices_between_pillars` regression test; suite 97/97 green.
